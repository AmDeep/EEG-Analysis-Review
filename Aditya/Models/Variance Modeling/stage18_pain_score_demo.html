Nurovo Pain Score Explorer

Each trial's EEG is measured twice: in the 1 s before the laser (baseline) and the 1 s after it (pain window). For 14 EEG metrics we take the change, weight it by how strongly that metric tracks laser intensity, and add everything up into one pain score. The score has no upper limit: 50 is a typical trial and every 10 points is one standard deviation, so a bigger response just reads higher.

Metric by metric

Baseline / In pain: the metric's value in each window, with its percentile (0-100) among all windows in the dataset. Change (z): pain minus baseline, divided by this subject's usual baseline spread. Contribution = weight × change (compared with a typical trial), so a metric that falls with intensity but has a negative weight still pushes the score up. Score = 50 + 10 × (sum − average sum) ÷ SD of the sum across all 29,515 trials. 50 is typical, +10 is one SD more intense; there is no ceiling or floor.

Does the score follow laser energy? Average score across all trials

The average rises with laser energy, but a single trial is noisy: the same energy produces a wide range of scores, which is why the "weak responder" examples exist. Most datasets here gave each person only one intensity, so part of this trend reflects differences between people and studies, not just one person's response.
