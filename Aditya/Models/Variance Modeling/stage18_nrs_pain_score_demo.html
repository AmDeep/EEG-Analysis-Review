Nurovo Pain Score Explorer · NRS-weighted

Each trial's EEG is measured twice: in the 1 s before the laser (baseline) and the 1 s after it (pain window). For 14 EEG metrics we take the change, weight it by how strongly that metric tracks the person's own reported pain (NRS, 0-10), and add everything up into one pain score. This is the NRS version of the explorer: every rank, sign and weight was fit to pain ratings, not laser energy. The score has no upper limit: 50 is a typical trial and every 10 points is one standard deviation, so a bigger response just reads higher.

Metric by metric

Baseline / In pain: the metric's value in each window, with its percentile (0-100) among all windows in the dataset. Change (z): pain minus baseline, divided by this subject's usual baseline spread. Weight comes from the metric's correlation with NRS; hover a row to compare with its laser correlation. Contribution = weight × change (compared with a typical trial), so a metric that falls with intensity but has a negative weight still pushes the score up. Score = 50 + 10 × (sum − average sum) ÷ SD of the sum across all rated trials. 50 is typical, +10 is one SD more intense; there is no ceiling or floor.

Does the score follow reported pain? Average score by NRS rating

Ratings are rounded to the nearest whole number for this curve. A single trial is noisy: the same rating comes with a wide range of scores, which is why the "weak responder" examples exist. Part of this trend reflects differences between people and studies, not just one person's response.

Does fitting to NRS help? Held-out subjects, agreement with NRS

5-fold cross-validation grouped by subject: weights are fit on 4/5 of the people and scored on the held-out 1/5. Across trials r is the correlation between score and NRS over all held-out trials. Within person ρ is the average per-person rank correlation, for people who gave at least 3 different ratings.
